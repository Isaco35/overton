1 · What the model measures: the Overton window

My readThe seat counts are a proxy. What this model is really tracking is the Overton window — the moving boundary of what is politically sayable and electable. Axis 2 (the {DS}-vs-{D} fight) is an Overton question: has "socialism" crossed from unsayable to electable? The window does not move because people are persuaded. It moves because the boundary of what gets punished at the ballot shifts. Every time an openly socialist candidate wins instead of loses, the warning "this will cost you" loses force — and the window slides. The general election is the test of how far it has slid. The decision tree (§11) is how we calibrate it: each dated, sourced event tells us which way the window moved.
window right: "socialism loses" window held: contested, NYC-bound window left a tick (where it sits 27 Jun) window left a step: socialism normalised
FactThe window has already moved a measurable tick: a self-described socialist holds the NYC mayoralty and froze rents on ~1M apartments (RGB vote, 25 Jun); three more {DS}/aligned candidates won safe-seat House primaries (23 Jun). Whether that tick becomes a step is what November decides.

13 · The median voter · the inattentive view

Fact — radicalized but not guidedThe political-science record supports a precise version of this. Rising "issue partisanship" is not higher ideological coherence — issue constraint has stayed "essentially stable, and low"; what looks like radicalization is mostly party sorting. Pew: majorities call both parties too extreme (GOP 61%, Dem 57%); frustration is bipartisan. Only 17% think Congress represents them; ~6 in 10 have no confidence the system solves problems.
My read — stress-testing "radicalized"The median voter is radicalized in affect (anger, anti-establishment heat) but not in ideology (no coherent program). The anger is a container, not a direction. That is exactly why the Overton window is up for grabs: whoever fills the container first — {DS} from the left or {RP} from the right — wins it, and both are competing to be the vehicle for the same "make the pain stop" demand. The window moves to whichever populism organises the anger faster.
